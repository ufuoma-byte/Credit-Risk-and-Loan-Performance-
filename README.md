Project Title:  
Credit Risk and Loan Performance 

Author: Ufuoma Precious Avworu

Project Overview:

This project leverages SQL for data querying and Power BI for visualization to analyze financial loan datasets. The goal is to uncover patterns and trends in loan approvals and credit risk using demographic, financial, and socio-economic factors. Insights generated aim to support smarter lending decisions, targeted credit management, and a deeper understanding of loan behaviors.

Project Objectives & Research Questions (RQ):

Objective 1: Understand the demographic influence on loan approval.  
- RQ1: Which demographic factors (gender, marital status, dependents, education) influence loan approval the most?

Objective 2: Analyze income and loan metrics to identify what financial profiles are likely to be approved.  
- RQ2: Does higher applicant or co-applicant income increase the chance of loan approval?  
- RQ3: What’s the relationship between loan amount and approval status?

Objective 3: Evaluate the impact of credit history on approval rates.
- RQ4: How does credit history affect loan approval?

Objective 4: Explore loan distribution across property areas and other socio-economic factors.  
- RQ5: Are there property areas more associated with loan denials?

Objective 5: Identify high-risk applicant profiles for targeted credit management.  
- RQ6: What are the characteristics of rejected applicants compared to approved ones?

Insights Breakdown:

1. Total Issued Loans & Loan Performance Summary:  
- Total loans: 38,576  
- Fully paid: 32,145  
- Charged-off (defaulted): 5,333  
- Current loans: 1,098  
- This shows a high repayment rate, but a significant portion (over 13%) still results in charge-offs, indicating credit risk pockets.

2. Loan Status by Purpose:  
- Visual reveals that loans for debt consolidation and credit card payments are most common.  
- Charged-offs are more frequent in these categories, suggesting these purposes carry higher risk.  
- Supports RQ6: Applicants with certain loan purposes are more likely to default.

3. Loan Volume by Issue Date & Monthly Trends:  
- There is a steady monthly increase in loan issuance throughout the year.
- Approval remains stable, though charged-offs increase slightly in later months—this can suggest seasonal credit strain or holiday spending.

4. Home Ownership vs Loan Performance:  
- Mortgage owners are the majority borrowers and show the highest rate of charge-offs.  
- Contrasts with renters, who represent fewer but better-performing loans.  
- Supports RQ5: Certain property ownership types correlate with loan denials or charge-offs.

5. Employment Length vs Charged-Off Loans:  
- Shorter employment length (0-1 years) is linked with the highest defaults.  
- Indicates that job stability is a critical risk factor.  
- Supports RQ6: Applicants with unstable employment are high-risk.

6. Annual Income & DTI Band Analysis:  
- Higher income ranges show better repayment performance.  
- Lower Debt-to-Income (DTI) bands correlate with lower defaults, proving a strong link between DTI and creditworthiness.  
- Strongly supports RQ2 and RQ3: Higher income and favorable DTI improve approval chances.

Key Findings Summary:

- Demographics like employment length and home ownership impact loan outcomes.  
- Income and DTI are clear indicators of approval chances.
- Loan purposes, especially debt-related, are more likely to default.  
- Risky applicant profiles can be identified for targeted credit control strategies.

Tools Used:

- SQL: Used for data cleaning, transformation, and querying key insights from the loan dataset. Queries were written to derive key metrics, such as default rates, DTI groupings, loan statuses, and income trends.  
- Power BI: For dynamic visualization of insights—clear, interactive charts to explore loan behaviors.

Challenges Encountered

During the course of this project, I encountered a few notable challenges:

1. SQL Query Design & Troubleshooting:  
One major hurdle was writing effective SQL queries. It wasn't just about being familiar with SQL syntax but ensuring that the queries returned the precise results needed for analysis. At several points, I faced logical issues or queries that didn’t run as expected. These moments felt like analytical blocks—similar to writer’s or creator’s block. To overcome this, I had to continually learn and refine my logic. I also leveraged tools like ChatGPT to help troubleshoot and reframe complex queries that were difficult to resolve alone.

2. Dashboard Design & Color Matching:  
Another challenge was related to dashboard aesthetics. I initially chose green as the primary theme color but struggled with selecting complementary colors that would maintain a professional and readable layout. To solve this, I explored online color palette resources to find shades that harmonized well with green. This helped ensure that the final dashboard looked visually appealing and coherent, rather than chaotic or unrefined
